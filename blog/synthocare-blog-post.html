How we built a disease-risk demo without using real patient records

A simple walkthrough of SynthoCare Risk Lab — synthetic chronic-disease data, machine-learning models, and a live Streamlit app you can try in the browser.

Written for students, developers, and health-tech learners · Easy language · No clinical claims

Important safety note: Everything described here is built on computer-generated (synthetic) data. The live app is for learning and demos only. It is not a medical device, not clinically validated, and must not be used to diagnose or treat real people.

Why this project exists

Real electronic health records are sensitive and hard to share. Students and engineers still need realistic practice data to learn how disease-risk models are built — without putting privacy at risk.

SynthoCare Risk Lab solves that by creating fake but realistic-looking patient-style records, training simple risk models on them, and putting the result behind a friendly web interface.

What diseases are covered?

The demo focuses on common long-term conditions (all labels are synthetic):

Type 2 diabetes Chronic kidney disease Coronary artery disease Heart failure Hypertension COPD Liver disease / MASLD Rheumatoid arthritis Hypothyroidism Cognitive decline

Models do not “see” future information. They only use things that would be available at a single visit idea: age, lifestyle, blood pressure, symptoms, family history, and optional lab values.

How it works (in plain English)

  1. Generate synthetic data. A script invents demographics, lifestyle risks, hidden disease “states,” labs that move over time, and delayed diagnoses — similar in spirit to real clinical patterns, but fully fake.
  2. Split by patient. People go into train / validation / test groups. The same synthetic person never appears in two groups. That prevents a common beginner mistake called data leakage.
  3. Train simple risk models. For each disease, the model learns patterns that tend to go with higher risk in the synthetic world (for example, higher HbA1c patterns for diabetes-like labels).
  4. Export a tiny “fast” model. Coefficients are saved as a small JSON file so the live website starts quickly without heavy libraries.
  5. Run the Streamlit app. Users fill a form (age, vitals, optional labs) and get multi-disease probability scores — clearly labeled as demo only.

What you will see in the live app

  • Home — project overview and safety disclaimer
  • Risk prediction — form to enter a scenario and run scores
  • Model metrics — how the models performed on held-out synthetic data
  • About & limits — what the tool is for and what it is not for

Think of the scores like a practice exam, not a doctor

High scores in the app mean: “In our synthetic training world, similar patterns often matched this label.” They do not mean a real person has that disease. Real care needs real clinicians and validated tools.

Key ideas we want learners to take away

1. Privacy first

Synthetic data lets teams practice without exposing real individuals.

2. Split carefully

Patient-level train/test splits reduce leakage and optimistic scores.

3. Metrics ≠ usefulness

Good accuracy on synthetic hold-out data does not prove clinical value.

4. Labels the UI clearly

Every serious health AI demo should state limits as loudly as features.

FAQ

Is any data from real hospitals?

No. All patient-style rows are generated by code for research and teaching.

Can doctors use this for patients?

No. It is not validated for care and is not intended for clinical decisions.

Why are there multiple disease scores?

Chronic conditions often co-exist. The demo shows multi-condition screening-style outputs, not a single diagnosis.

Why is the website sometimes slow?

Free Streamlit hosting can cold-start after idle time or throttle CPU under load. Local runs are usually faster and more stable.

Can I reuse the code?

Yes — the project is open on GitHub (MIT-style open license with a clear non-clinical notice). Please keep the synthetic/disclaimer messaging if you remix it.

Closing thought

Healthcare AI education needs safe sandboxes. SynthoCare Risk Lab is one such sandbox: realistic enough to practice pipelines and product thinking, honest enough to say “synthetic only — not for patient care.”

If you try the live demo, treat every score as a teaching example. The real future of clinical AI still depends on rigorous validation with real-world data, ethics review, and clinical governance.
